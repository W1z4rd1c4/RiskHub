# ICT Register list and ownership consistency — grilling capture

_Status: confirmed 2026-07-15. Shared understanding is complete and implementation is authorized in the existing `dora` worktree._

## Objective

Bring Processes, Assets, Threats, and the related Vendor experience to the established RiskHub list-page, ownership, localization, and visual-interaction standard used by Risks and Controls. Include adjacent defects discovered within that scope.

## Locked decisions

1. **Target** — design and eventual implementation target the existing `dora` worktree; preserve its current uncommitted work.
2. **Language contract** — controlled register values have locale-independent meaning and locale-specific labels. English UI shows English labels and Czech UI shows Czech labels. Workbook import maps source labels to canonical values; regulatory export may reproduce required source terminology. User-entered free text is not translated.
3. **Responsibility cardinality** — every responsibility role has exactly one Accountable owner linked to an active RiskHub User. Department ownership is separate. Do not model co-owners, participants, contributors, or watchers.
4. **Threat responsibility** — every Threat has one Threat Steward. The Steward must be an active User holding the CISO role; this eligibility rule is enforced by the backend and reflected by the picker.
5. **CISO role** — add a first-class `ciso` role with focused security-governance access: full Threat lifecycle and Threat–Risk link management; read Risks, Controls, Issues, Processes, Assets, Vendors, vendor contracts, Departments, reports, ICT Committee, and activity history; no user/platform administration, approvals, or unrestricted writes to the other registers.
6. **Demo availability** — seed a CISO demo user and expose that persona in the demo login picker. Replace the current three grouped panels with ten equal persona cards in a desktop five-column × two-row grid; each card retains its role and department context.
7. **Responsibility map** — Process has one Process Owner; Asset has distinct Business Owner and ICT Owner roles; Vendor retains one Outsourcing Owner; Threat has one CISO-only Threat Steward; existing Risk, Control, and KRI responsibility models remain unchanged. Do not replace these precise roles with a generic "Responsible User" field.
8. **Asset role overlap** — Business Owner and ICT Owner remain separate responsibility roles, but the same active User may hold both roles for one Asset.
9. **Required ownership** — every newly created active Process, Asset, Vendor, and Threat must have all responsibility roles assigned; forms and backend validation enforce the rule.
10. **No owner-data migration** — treat the ownership change as a pre-release model correction. Add the required schema change, but do not map or preserve legacy free-text owner values and do not build an owner-reconciliation workflow. Rebuild the demo/E2E ICT Register seed around the demo User records and update affected E2E expectations.
11. **Clean ownership model** — replace Process and Asset free-text owner fields with User relationships and add the CISO-constrained Threat Steward relationship. Expose safe user display metadata in reads. Do not retain, dual-write, or fall back to legacy owner text.
12. **Canonical Departments** — replace Process and Asset owner-department text with one Owning Department relationship to an active RiskHub Department. Forms, lists, and filters use the canonical department directory; no free-text fallback remains.
13. **Owner/department coupling** — selecting a Process Owner or Asset Business Owner auto-fills the user's Department only when Owning Department is empty. The Department remains independently editable, may differ from the owner's Department, and is validated only as active. Asset ICT Owner may belong to another Department.
14. **Owner lifecycle** — extend RiskHub's orphan-governance workflow to Process Owner, both Asset owner roles, Vendor Outsourcing Owner, and Threat Steward. Deactivation never silently clears/replaces ownership; loss of CISO eligibility also orphans affected Threat stewardship. Preserve the previous assignee for audit, exclude ineligible users from new selections, and require explicit governance reassignment.
15. **Filter/list interaction** — every register follows the established Risks-page process: header, entity-relevant view buttons, shared filter bar, grouped drill-down, sortable table, and pagination. Search/status stay visible; a Filters control adds visible field controls/chips with active count, individual removal, and Clear all. Do not copy irrelevant view/filter buttons and do not use an off-screen drawer.
16. **Department operational view** — Department detail exposes Overview, Risks, Controls, KRIs, Issues, Processes, Assets, Vendors, Users, and Activity tabs. Entity tabs select records by canonical Owning Department even when the individual owner belongs elsewhere. Threats remain global under CISO stewardship and have no Department tab. The summary/grid may be redesigned to fit the expanded content.
17. **Department Overview** — use eight clickable entity cards in a four-column × two-row desktop grid: Risks, Controls, KRIs, Issues, Processes, Assets, Vendors, and Users. Each card includes a relevant secondary health count and opens its tab with that health filter when applicable. Recent Activity remains full-width below the grid.
18. **Process list controls** — view buttons are All, By Department, By Owner, By L0 Area, By Criticality, and By Vendor. Addable filters are lifecycle status, Owning Department, Process Owner, L0 Area, criticality class, CIF status, completeness, licensed activity, BCM linkage, DR test result, MTPD range, Linked Asset, Linked Vendor, and Linked Risk. Search covers F-code, L0/L1/L2 names, owner name, and department.
19. **Asset list controls** — view buttons are All, By Department, By Business Owner, By Type, By Criticality, By Process, and By Vendor. Addable filters are lifecycle/archive status, Owning Department, Business Owner, ICT Owner, asset type/level, deployment model, resulting criticality, CIF support, lifecycle/legacy state, SPOF, external dependency, GDPR/AI relevance, internet exposure, data classification, completeness, and Linked Process/Asset/Vendor/Risk. Search covers name, alternative names, type, both owners, department, and location.
20. **Threat list controls** — view buttons are All, By Category, By Threat Steward, By Relevant Subject, and By Linked Risk. Addable filters are lifecycle status, category, Threat Steward, relevant subject, linked-risk presence, Linked Risk, Linked Risk type, and Linked Risk department. Search covers name, description, typical weaknesses, relevant subject, and steward. By Linked Risk uses multi-membership grouping across every readable linked Risk.
21. **Vendor list controls** — retain All, By Department, By Process, By Type, By Risk, and By Flag views. Addable filters are lifecycle status, Owning Department, Outsourcing Owner, vendor type/risk score/tier, DORA/CIF/significant flags, substitutability, country/category, RoI-contract presence, sub-outsourcing presence, direct-Process-link presence, and Linked Process/Asset/Risk/Control/KRI. Search covers vendor/legal name, registration identifier, owner, department, and process.
22. **Shared register scope** — normalize Risks, Controls, KRIs, Issues, Vendors, Processes, Assets, and Threats on the same filter/list framework, including active-filter chips, clear behavior, URL state, loading/error states, and permission-scoped server semantics. Preserve useful existing quick filters. Department tabs reuse the same query/list components with a locked Department filter rather than separate miniature tables.
23. **Filter semantics** — AND across different fields, OR across multiple selected values within one field, with search additionally ANDed. Booleans expose Yes/No/Any; numeric/date ranges are inclusive. Grouping operates on the filtered universe, filter changes reset to page 1, and URL state preserves search, view, sort, filters, selected group, and browser navigation.
24. **Facet and lookup contract** — filter options and counts are backend-driven from the caller's visible universe. Controlled values use canonical codes and localized labels, with valid zero-result values disabled. User and linked-entity choices use remote searchable lookups, selected IDs remain resolvable across paging, and neither metadata nor counts may leak hidden records.
25. **Register-list visual contract** — Risks and Vendors are the visual/interaction source of truth. All eight registers consume one shared shell for header typography/rhythm, Export/New action placement, view switcher, filter card, grouped/all table presentation, density, badges, archive/restore treatment, async/empty states, and pagination. Entity-specific JSX supplies configuration/content rather than alternate layout.
26. **Filtered exports** — all eight register lists provide capability-gated export of every row matching current search, filters, and selected group, independent of pagination and under the same permission scope. Standard exports localize controlled labels to the user's locale and retain stable codes; the formal RoI export remains a separate regulatory action with mandated structure/terminology.
27. **Controlled-value reset** — replace stored Czech controlled labels with canonical codes, rebuild demo/E2E seeds and fixtures, and keep no legacy database-label fallback. Workbook import explicitly maps source labels to codes; regulatory export maps codes to mandated terminology.
28. **Form/detail UX scope** — extend RiskHub visual consistency to Process, Asset, and Threat create/edit/detail pages: established headers/back behavior, section cards, field primitives, spacing, actions, tabs, dialogs, ownership/Department pickers, localized controlled selects, and Risk/Vendor-style link sections. Preserve domain-appropriate field grouping rather than forcing every entity into the Risk wizard.
29. **Owner edit authority** — Process Owner, either Asset owner, Vendor Outsourcing Owner, and CISO Threat Steward may edit their assigned active record; Owning Department Head may edit that Department's Processes and Assets. Backend per-row capabilities are authoritative and mutations are audited. Archive/restore remains restricted to Risk Manager/CRO-level authority. Critical/important Process, Asset, and Vendor mutations additionally require an approval workflow whose trigger and scope remain to be resolved.
30. **Protected-record threshold** — a Process is protected when derived CIF is Yes; an Asset is protected when derived CIF is Yes or resulting criticality is Critical; a Vendor is protected when its derived tier is Critical or Significant. Evaluate both current and proposed post-change state so classification inputs cannot be changed first to bypass approval.
31. **Protected mutation scope** — protected-record approval covers creation, every business-data update, relationship/link addition or removal, and archiving. A protected creation does not become active until approval; for an existing record, the approved state remains effective until the pending mutation is approved. Comments, evidence attachments, and activity notes do not trigger approval. Restore remains a privileged direct action because it reinstates a previously approved state.
32. **Protected approval authority** — every protected mutation requires one approval from an eligible Risk Manager or CRO. Enforce strict requester/approver separation: a requester can never approve their own request, and neither Risk Manager nor CRO can bypass approval when requesting a protected mutation. Owners and Department Heads may submit changes but are not approvers by virtue of those responsibilities.
33. **Pending-mutation concurrency** — allow at most one pending business mutation per Process, Asset, or Vendor. While it is pending, block later business-state edits and relationship/link mutations for that record, but continue to allow comments and evidence. The requester may cancel their request. At approval time, revalidate requester and approver authorization, owner/Department eligibility, referenced entities, derived classifications, and the record version. If the approved base state or any required invariant is stale, expire the request without applying it and require a fresh submission.
34. **Pending-state UX** — approval state is separate from lifecycle state. Existing records retain their factual Active/Archived lifecycle and show a distinct Pending change badge in register lists and Department tabs. Detail pages show a pending banner and read-only field/relationship diff. Approval review shows entity code/name, requester, mandatory request reason, timestamp, before/after values, derived CIF/criticality/tier impact, and relationship changes; rejection requires a reason. Proposed values and referenced entity labels remain subject to the reviewer's visibility scope.
35. **Pending creation visibility** — a protected creation is a non-operational proposal visible only to its requester and eligible approvers through a Pending creation row in the relevant register and through My Requests/Approvals. Exclude it from Department health counts, facets, standard exports, relationships, and other users' operational lists. Approval atomically creates the active record; rejection or cancellation retains only immutable approval/audit history.
36. **Accountability reassignment** — changing Process Owner, Asset Business Owner, Asset ICT Owner, Vendor Outsourcing Owner, Threat Steward, or a Process/Asset Owning Department always requires one independent Risk Manager/CRO approval, even when the record is not protected. Authorized editors may request an atomic reassignment but cannot directly clear or transfer accountability. The standard requester/approver separation, pending-state, revalidation, and audit rules apply.
37. **Assignment-derived access** — an accountable assignment grants the assignee read access and the previously defined edit capability for that specific record even when ordinary Department scope would hide it. It may expose permission-safe summaries of directly linked entities needed to understand the assigned record, but does not grant general register access or unrestricted access to linked records. Department Heads retain Department-scoped Process/Asset access only.
38. **Responsibility picker eligibility** — any active RiskHub User may be selected as Process Owner, Asset Business Owner, Asset ICT Owner, or Vendor Outsourcing Owner without requiring an additional RBAC role. Pickers support cross-Department remote search and disambiguate results with name, email, Department, and assigned roles. Threat Steward remains restricted to active CISO users.
39. **Inherited register defaults** — when the URL contains no explicit state, all normalized registers open in All view with active records and no user-selected sort, matching the mature Risks/Vendors controller contract. Backend business-key ordering remains the deterministic fallback. Department tabs add their locked Department constraint without changing these defaults.
40. **Cascade-aware approval** — approval follows the full Process → Asset → Vendor derivation cascade. If a Process, Asset, Vendor contract, sub-outsourcing, or relationship mutation would create or alter a protected downstream state, submit one composite request that explains every derived impact. Lock the primary and affected records while pending, rederive and revalidate the complete impact set at decision time, and apply the mutation atomically; never partially apply a cascade.
41. **No approval SLA** — do not add due dates, reminders, overdue labels, or time-based escalation for Process/Asset/Vendor governance approvals. A request remains pending until it is explicitly approved, rejected, cancelled, or expired because its validated base state became stale. Preserve ordinary event notifications associated with submission and resolution, but introduce no timer-driven workflow.
42. **Approval-control settings surface** — expose the new Process/Asset/Vendor protected-mutation and responsibility-reassignment controls in Risk Hub settings rather than hard-coding an invisible policy. The exact permitted configuration, including whether approval may be disabled, remains open.
43. **Configurable approval scenarios** — add four fixed Risk Hub approval scenarios: Protected Process mutations, Protected Asset mutations, Protected Vendor mutations, and Accountability reassignments. Each defaults enabled; CRO may enable/disable each scenario and select Risk Manager, CRO, or both as eligible approver roles. Protected thresholds and mutation scope remain fixed, and requester/approver separation can never be disabled. Demo/E2E configuration enables all four with both roles.
44. **Approval notification preferences** — represent submission and outcome events from these workflows in each User's notification settings. Exact preference granularity and recipients remain to be resolved; the earlier no-SLA decision still excludes timed reminders and overdue escalation.
45. **Approval notification categories** — add two default-on per-User preferences: Approval requests requiring my action (submission, cancellation, or stale expiry for eligible approvers) and Updates to my approval requests (approval, rejection, cancellation, or stale outcome for the requester). Disabling a preference suppresses only event notifications; it never removes the request from Approvals/My Requests or suppresses unread-work counts. Do not add SLA reminders.
46. **Verification and documentation definition of done** — completion requires backend coverage for schema/seed, ownership RBAC, scoped facets, filter/export semantics, approval configuration, cascade atomicity, concurrency/staleness, notifications, localization, and Postgres transactions; frontend coverage for the shared shell, URL state, pickers, Department surfaces, approval UX, notification settings, localization, and ten-persona demo picker; E2E coverage for CISO stewardship, ownership, protected workflows, no-self-approval, Department drill-down, export, and representative filters across all eight registers; and the canonical backend, frontend, type, i18n, authorization-contract, architecture-lock, Playwright, and visual-comparison gates. Update every affected canonical application document and end-user/admin manual in the same change so code, contracts, workflows, setup/demo guidance, and screenshots remain synchronized.
